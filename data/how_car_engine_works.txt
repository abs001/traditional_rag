HOW A CAR ENGINE WORKS (THE BASICS)

Most cars use an Internal Combustion Engine. This means fuel burns inside the engine to create power. 

The heart of the engine is the CYLINDER. Inside the cylinder is a moving part called a PISTON. The piston moves up and down.

Most car engines use a FOUR-STROKE CYCLE. This happens in four quick steps:

1. INTAKE: The piston moves down. A valve opens to let fuel and air into the cylinder.
2. COMPRESSION: The piston moves back up. It squeezes (compresses) the fuel and air mixture tightly.
3. POWER: A spark plug creates a tiny spark. This ignites the fuel, causing a small explosion. The force pushes the piston down rapidly. This creates the engine's power.
4. EXHAUST: The piston moves back up. Another valve opens to let the leftover burned gas out of the engine through the tailpipe.

TURNING UP-AND-DOWN INTO FORWARD MOTION
As the pistons pump up and down, they turn a heavy rod called the CRANKSHAFT. Think of it like a cyclist's legs turning the pedals of a bicycle. The crankshaft converts the straight up-and-down motion into a spinning motion. This spinning energy is sent to your wheels to make the car move forward.

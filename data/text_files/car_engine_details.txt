HOW A CAR ENGINE WORKS: AN INTRODUCTORY GUIDE

Most modern gasoline cars use an internal combustion engine that operates on a four-stroke cycle. This process converts the chemical energy in fuel into mechanical motion to drive the vehicle's wheels.

=========================================
THE FOUR-STROKE CYCLE (THE OTTO CYCLE)
=========================================

1. INTAKE STROKE
   - The piston moves downward inside the cylinder.
   - The intake valve opens.
   - A mixture of fresh air and fuel is drawn into the cylinder.

2. COMPRESSION STROKE
   - The intake and exhaust valves both close, sealing the cylinder.
   - The piston moves back upward.
   - This motion tightly squeezes (compresses) the air-fuel mixture, making the upcoming explosion significantly more powerful.

3. POWER (COMBUSTION) STROKE
   - The air-fuel mixture is fully compressed.
   - The spark plug emits an electric spark.
   - This ignites the fuel, causing a controlled explosion.
   - The rapid expansion of burning gases forces the piston down with great force.
   - This downward force rotates the crankshaft, generating the car's power.

4. EXHAUST STROKE
   - The exhaust valve opens.
   - The piston moves upward once more.
   - It pushes the spent exhaust gases out of the cylinder and into the exhaust system.
   - The cycle then repeats thousands of times per minute.

=========================================
KEY ENGINE COMPONENTS AND THEIR ROLES
=========================================

- CYLINDER: The metal tube or chamber where the combustion process takes place. Most cars have 4, 6, or 8 cylinders.
- PISTON: A tight-fitting metal plug that slides up and down inside the cylinder, driven by the expanding gases.
- CRANKSHAFT: A heavy, rotating shaft beneath the cylinders. It converts the linear up-and-down motion of the pistons into rotational motion that turns the wheels.
- CONNECTING ROD: The steel rod linking each piston to the crankshaft.
- SPARK PLUG: A device at the top of the cylinder that delivers the electrical spark needed to ignite the fuel.
- VALVES: Mechanical gates (intake and exhaust) that open and close at precise intervals to manage air-fuel entry and exhaust exit.
- CAMSHAFT: A rotating shaft with lobes (cams) that opens and closes the valves in perfect synchronization with the pistons.

=========================================
SUPPORTING SYSTEMS
=========================================

- FUEL SYSTEM: Pumps gasoline or diesel from the tank and atomizes it into the air intake or directly into the cylinder.
- COOLING SYSTEM: Circulates coolant (antifreeze and water) around the engine block to dissipate the massive heat generated by combustion.
- LUBRICATION SYSTEM: Pumps oil through the engine to coat moving parts, reducing friction and preventing wear.
